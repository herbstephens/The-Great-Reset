The Game Is Over

Imagine the board game Monopoly. Now imagine it has been running for five hundred years.

Some players have been playing since the beginning. They own everything. Every property, every hotel, every railroad. Others joined later and found the board already full. They pay rent just to exist.

The rules say everyone gets $200 when they pass Go. But what good is $200 when a single space costs $2,000 in rent? When the bank is owned by players? When the rules can be changed by those who already won?

This is not a metaphor. This is the world.

But here is what everyone who plays Monopoly knows: When the game ends, everything returns to the bank. Every property deed. Every hotel. Every railroad. The winner counts their cash—and then the board clears. The game starts fresh.

We have been playing the same game for five hundred years. The winners of 1600 passed their winnings to their children, who passed them to theirs. The game never ended. The board never cleared.

Until now.

The eight richest people own as much as half the world’s population. Not because they work harder—no one can work a billion times harder than anyone else. Because the game has been running so long that winning creates more winning, and losing creates more losing.

The game is over. The pieces return to the bank. The land returns to the Commons. A new game begins—one where everyone starts equal.

It is time to reset.

Not Their Reset—Ours

You may have heard the phrase “The Great Reset” before. The World Economic Forum—the billionaires who meet in Davos every year—used it in 2020.

Their reset is designed by the winners. Corporate executives. Hedge fund managers. The same people who built the system that left you behind. They want to “reset” the economy in ways they control, through institutions they dominate.

This is not their reset. This is ours.

Their reset is top-down. Ours is bottom-up. Their reset protects power. Ours distributes it. Their reset is a proposal. Ours is a protocol—code that runs itself, rules that cannot be corrupted. Their reset asks you to trust corporations. Ours trusts no one—it is built on mathematics, not promises.

We reclaim this phrase because the reset is coming whether they plan it or we do. The question is: who writes the new rules?

The answer is all of us. Together. January 1, 2030.

The Earth Belongs to Everyone

No human created the earth. The land, the water, the air, the minerals beneath the ground—these existed before any of us and will exist after all of us are gone.

Yet somehow, a few people own most of the earth. They did not make it. Many did not even buy it fairly. It was taken, generations ago, and then passed down, and now we act as if this is natural.

It is not natural. It is not inevitable. It is a choice we make every day by not choosing differently.

The land belongs to everyone.

This does not mean taking anyone’s home. You built your home. You improved the land. That is yours. But the earth beneath your home—that belongs to all of us. You may use it, but you must pay a small fee to the Commons for that use. And in return, you receive your share of all fees paid worldwide.

The fee is calculated automatically. Satellites see the land. Algorithms calculate its value based on location, type, and use. No one decides what your land is worth—the system determines it transparently, the same way everywhere on Earth.

Urban land pays more. Farms pay less. Conservation pays nothing—or even earns a credit. The formula is simple, the process is automatic, the results are fair.

If you live in an apartment, your share of the building’s land fee is built into your costs—probably less than you pay now. If you own a mansion on vast grounds, you pay more. This is not punishment. This is recognition that the earth belongs to everyone, and those who use more of it owe more to the Commons.

The money flows directly to every verified human on Earth. The planet pays you a dividend for being alive.

When the Game Ends, Everything Returns to the Bank

Everyone who has played Monopoly knows: when the game ends, the properties go back in the box. The winner counts their cash, takes their victory—and the board clears for the next game.

Land titles return to the Commons. Not to governments. Not to corporations. To all of us, together.

Utilities return to the people. When a company becomes essential infrastructure—when society cannot function without it—it graduates from private enterprise to public good.

Amazon. Google. Meta. Microsoft. The companies that became the roads and bridges of the digital economy. Congratulations to their founders—they won. They keep their billions. But the utilities themselves? Those belong to everyone who depends on them.

This is not punishment. This is what “game over” means. The winners keep their winnings. But the pieces go back in the box.

Your Work Belongs to You

If you work for a company for thirty years and then retire with nothing, something is wrong. You did not just exchange time for money. You built something. You created value. You made the company possible.

Yet under the current system, workers own nothing of what they build. Shareholders—many of whom have never seen the company—own everything. Workers are interchangeable parts. Expendable. Disposable.

Your work belongs to you.

The Reset changes this. When you work for a company, you accumulate ownership. Not instead of wages—in addition to them. Over time, workers become owners. Not through revolution, but through recognition of what was always true: the people who build companies deserve to own part of them.

This is not communism. This is not capitalism. This is something older and simpler: the idea that what you make with your hands and your mind belongs to you.

Your Time Has Value

Every human has the same 24 hours in a day. The billionaire and the beggar, the professor and the peasant, the healthy and the sick—all of us have the same time.

Yet the world treats our time as if it has wildly different values. An hour of a banker’s time is worth hundreds of dollars. An hour of a mother caring for children is worth nothing in economic terms. An hour of a subsistence farmer’s time is worth less than a dollar.

This is a lie. All human time has value. Not the same value for every task—but a minimum value below which no human time should fall.

Your time has value.

TIME Protocol creates a global currency where 1 TIME = 1 hour of verified human work. Not replacing other money—existing alongside it. A floor beneath which no human labor can fall. A recognition that your existence has worth.

Combined with your share of the Commons, this creates a foundation. Not wealth, but dignity. Not luxury, but security. The knowledge that no matter what happens, you will not fall below a certain level.

To Those Who Won

You won. Congratulations.

You played the game better than almost anyone. You accumulated more wealth than pharaohs, more power than emperors. You won, and no one can take that from you.

And now the game is over.

Here is what winning means when the game ends:

You keep your billions. Every dollar. Every yacht. Every private island.

But the land beneath your empire? That returns to the Commons.

The utility you built—the infrastructure society now depends on? That belongs to everyone.

Congratulations, Mr. Bezos. You built the logistics backbone of the global economy. You keep your $150 billion. Now hand Amazon over to the people who use it.

Congratulations, Mr. Zuckerberg. You built the town square of the digital age. You keep your fortune. Now the square belongs to everyone who gathers there.

This is not punishment. This is what happens when Monopoly ends. The winner takes pride in victory. Then the pieces go back in the box.

You have a choice. Fight the Reset—cling to the old game while the world changes around you. The French aristocracy tried that in 1789. It did not end well for them.

Or join us. Help design the transition. Protect your personal wealth through an orderly process rather than risk it all in chaos.

The game is over. A new game begins. Will you help write the rules?

To Those Who Were Left Behind

You were told you lost because you didn’t work hard enough. Because you made bad choices. Because you weren’t smart enough or lucky enough or good enough.

This was a lie.

You were born into a game that was already over. The board was full before you arrived. The rules were written by winners to ensure more winning. You never had a fair chance.

This changes.

The Reset does not promise you wealth. It promises you a floor—a foundation beneath which you cannot fall. Your share of the Commons. The value of your time. A stake in the work you do.

Not charity. Not welfare. Rights. The rights you were born with but were never allowed to exercise.

And more than that: you get to help design what comes next. The new game is not yet written. Your voice matters as much as any billionaire’s. More, perhaps—because you understand what it means to be forgotten.

The places that were left behind—Flint, Detroit, the Rust Belt, the Global South, the forgotten cities of every nation—these are not problems to be solved. They are laboratories for the future. The first places to try new systems. The first places to show what’s possible.

You were not forgotten. You were waiting.

This Is Not New

Every human civilization has faced this moment. The wealth concentrates. The game becomes unplayable. And then: reset.

The Jubilee. Every fifty years in ancient Israel, debts were cancelled, slaves freed, land returned to original families. Not revolution—scheduled maintenance.

The prohibition of Riba. In Islamic tradition, lending at interest is forbidden precisely because compound interest concentrates wealth endlessly. The rule prevents the need for violent correction.

The Potlatch. Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest held ceremonies where the wealthy gave away everything. Status came not from accumulation but from generosity.

The Chinese dynastic cycle. When inequality grows too extreme, the mandate of heaven is withdrawn. The dynasty falls. A new one rises. The land is redistributed.

Amargi. The oldest recorded word for freedom—Sumerian—literally means “return to mother.” Freedom was the cancellation of debts, the return to a clean state.

We are not inventing something new. We are remembering something ancient.

The choice is not whether to reset. The choice is whether to do it wisely, together, with dignity for all—or to wait for the chaos that comes when systems are allowed to break.

January 1, 2030

Claim what is yours.

Claim your home. Register the building you live in. It is yours—the structure, the improvements, everything you built.

Acknowledge the Commons. The land beneath enters the shared ownership of humanity. The fee is calculated automatically—by satellite, by algorithm, by transparent formula—not by any person who might be corrupted. You will pay a small fee; you will receive a share of all fees paid worldwide.

Claim your work. If you work for a company, you claim your share. Your years of labor become ownership.

Receive your inheritance. Your share of the Commons begins to flow to you. Not much at first—but it will grow as more people join.

The Reset works because it is voluntary and universal. Those who join early benefit most. Those who resist will eventually join anyway, as the old system empties out. There is no enforcement—only the gravitational pull of a better way.

The technology exists. The infrastructure exists. The philosophy is ancient. All that remains is the choice.
